# Narrated scenes with a shared visual style

Use for requested multi-scene narration, especially educational or animated work.
This is a planning and generation method, not a dedicated film assembler. Confirm
an available finishing path before promising a single edited MP4; no automatic
composition command is implied. Ordinary one-clip requests skip this branch.

## Shared style and references

Write one reusable description of medium, palette, line/fill behavior and finish.
For example: hand-painted gouache, warm muted colors, soft brush texture. Apply
non-photoreal constraints only to illustrated work, not every narrated video.

If style drift is a material risk and an existing suitable reference is unavailable,
a style-key image may help. Do not require an additional paid image by default.
Choose a supported endpoint and reference role; not all models can independently
control style, identity and composition.

Abstract key template:

```text
A visual style sample in {medium}, showing {line quality}, {fill behavior},
{edge/highlight treatment}, {texture} and {palette}. No narrative scene or text.
```

Recurring character key template:

```text
{Shared style}. {Character description}, full body, readable silhouette,
simple background, consistent clothing and identifying features.
```

A style donor supplies rendering and color grading only. Do not import its people,
words, logos or objects unless those are explicitly requested, permitted inputs.

## Match narration and picture

Research factual topics using relevant sources; distinguish product claims from
verified facts. Personal stories use supplied details without invented facts.
For every scene, pair one spoken beat with one visible event or explanation:

```text
Scene number:
Shared style:
Narration: actual spoken words, in the requested language.
Visual: one event or visible proof corresponding to those words.
Motion: subject action and camera behavior.
Audio: narration source, environmental sound and any speech generated in the clip.
Continuity: required identity, product, setting and reference roles.
Duration: actual speech timing and supported video bounds.
Results: completed audio and video references; missing outputs remain missing.
```

Keep spoken text separate from stage directions. Choose pronunciation-friendly
wording for numbers when needed. Do not impose a fixed English word count or ten
seconds on every language and model. Listen to or measure real takes before
claiming timing fits. If narration is supplied separately, avoid accidental second
narration in the video; this is not a blanket ban on dialogue or lip-sync.

## Checkpoints

Reuse the chosen voice and style where continuity matters. Before a dependent
video request, require its actual completed input assets. Before any available
assembly step, check ordered scene/audio pairing with no missing or duplicate
outputs and verify final timing. Do not replace result IDs with planned filenames.

An interrupted wait resumes the original operation. For a defective completed
scene, describe the defect and any proposed regeneration within the user's
existing authorization; do not automatically rerender a whole film or hide cost.
Delivery distinguishes finished scenes from a finished assembled film.
